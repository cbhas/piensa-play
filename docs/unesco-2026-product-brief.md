# PiensaPlay — UNESCO Youth Hackathon 2026 product brief

## One-line proposal

PiensaPlay is an offline-capable bilingual game that helps children aged 8–12 turn media and information literacy into a repeatable everyday habit.

## Problem

Children encounter viral claims, AI-generated images and social pressure to share before they have a simple way to slow down and evaluate what they see. Many learning resources explain misinformation after the fact; fewer let children practise a decision, see its social consequence and transfer the skill to a new situation.

## Solution

Players enter a Digital City whose trust has been damaged. Across short scenario-based missions they use the **PIENSA** routine:

1. **Pause** before reacting.
2. **Identify** who created the message and what they want.
3. **Examine** the claim and its context.
4. **Notice** emotional pressure, manipulation and possible harm.
5. **Seek** independent evidence or a trusted adult.
6. **Act** by sharing responsibly, correcting or choosing not to amplify.

The flagship prototype includes three complete missions: a viral school alert, an AI-generated image and the community ripple caused by sharing. Each decision includes evidence clues, choice-specific feedback and a real-life takeaway.

## Audience and settings

- Primary learners: children aged 8–12.
- Secondary users: teachers, librarians, youth facilitators and families.
- Initial contexts: schools and community learning spaces in Latin America, with Spanish/English international access.
- Designed for individual play, shared-device classroom voting and unreliable connectivity.

## Innovation and creativity

- A memorable Spanish acronym becomes a transferable critical-thinking scaffold.
- Trust, not popularity, is the central game resource.
- Classroom mode turns a phone or projector into a structured group discussion.
- Baseline and transfer questions measure decision quality without asking children for identifying information.
- The same content model supports future culturally reviewed language and scenario packs.

## Feasibility

The working Flutter prototype targets Android, iOS and web from one codebase. The flagship content runs locally; Firebase is used for optional synchronization and catalogue updates. A facilitator can run a 30-minute session using one projected device, so adoption does not depend on every learner owning a phone.

## Sustainability

- Open, modular content structure lowers the cost of adding missions and translations.
- Facilitator-led delivery supports schools, libraries and youth organizations.
- Future sustainability options include institutional sponsorship, teacher toolkits and public-interest partnerships—never behavioural advertising to children.
- Aggregate pilot evidence can guide content investment and support grant applications.

## Impact and inclusion

The prototype records anonymous first-choice decisions and optional baseline/post responses locally. Proposed pilot indicators are:

- improvement from baseline to transfer score;
- percentage choosing a verification action on the first attempt;
- mission completion;
- facilitator-rated clarity and relevance;
- accessibility or language barriers reported by learners.

No impact percentage should be published until a supervised pilot has been completed. Results must be aggregated, and small groups must not be reported in a way that could identify a child.

## Alignment with the call

PiensaPlay directly addresses **MIL Education** and **AI and MIL**, with a community-delivery model. It is a working game/application, aligns with peace, inclusion and responsible expression, and is designed around the published judging dimensions: theme fit, clarity, innovation, feasibility, sustainability, impact and inclusion.

## Pilot-to-scale plan

1. Run moderated sessions with 20–40 learners in two contexts.
2. Revise unclear language and choices using observation and child feedback.
3. Add teacher export of aggregate, privacy-preserving learning evidence.
4. Co-design one new scenario with each partner community.
5. Validate additional languages with educators and native speakers before release.
