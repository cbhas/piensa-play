# Facilitator and pilot guide

## 30-minute learning session

### Before learners arrive — 5 minutes

- Open the demo and choose Spanish or English.
- Enable classroom mode if learners will share a screen.
- State the agreement: discuss messages, never expose a classmate's private experience.
- Make clear that the activity is not a test of intelligence and that learners may pass.

### Baseline — 3 minutes

Invite learners to answer the optional three-item baseline independently. Do not coach or reveal the later scenarios. If devices are shared, collect a private show of hands or paper response and enter only aggregate counts.

### Play and discuss — 15 minutes

For each challenge:

1. Read the scenario aloud.
2. Give silent thinking time.
3. Ask groups to vote before revealing the result.
4. Invite two groups to cite a clue—not only state an opinion.
5. Reveal the feedback and connect it to one PIENSA step.

Useful prompts: “Who benefits if we believe this?”, “What evidence is missing?”, “Who could be harmed if we share?” and “What is the smallest safe action we can take now?”

### Transfer and close — 7 minutes

Complete the optional transfer questions. Ask every learner to name one PIENSA action they can use this week. Close with a trusted-adult reminder: difficult, threatening or sexual content should not be investigated alone.

## Inclusion adaptations

- Read content aloud and allow partner discussion.
- Use larger text and reduced motion when helpful.
- Explain unfamiliar platform terms without assuming prior access to social media.
- Let learners respond verbally, by pointing or on paper.
- Avoid asking learners to disclose bullying, harassment or misinformation they personally experienced.
- Add languages only after translation and scenario review with native speakers and local educators.

## Minimum ethical pilot protocol

1. Obtain approval from the school or organization and guardian consent/child assent when required locally.
2. Give families a plain-language information sheet describing purpose, data and withdrawal.
3. Assign anonymous session codes; do not collect names, accounts, phone numbers or social handles for research.
4. Record only the minimum: age band, aggregate baseline/transfer score, mission completion and optional feedback.
5. Keep a facilitator incident and safeguarding contact plan.
6. Delete raw pilot data on a documented schedule and publish only aggregated results.

## Evidence template

Report the number invited, number participating, setting, language, device model, completion, baseline/transfer distributions, first-choice verification rate, accessibility observations and limitations. Separate usability findings from learning outcomes. A small pilot supports iteration; it does not establish long-term behaviour change.
